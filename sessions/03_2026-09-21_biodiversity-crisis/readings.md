# Readings, session 3 (21 September 2026)

## Required

**Díaz, S., et al. (2019).** Pervasive human-driven decline of life on Earth points to the need for
transformative change. *Science*, 366(6471), eaax3100.
<https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax3100>

The IPBES synthesis in article form, and the core reading for this session.

> Published by AAAS under "some rights reserved", with no Creative Commons license, so the PDF is
> not in this repository. RUC students reach it through the university library, and registered
> students also find it on Moodle. Several of the authors have deposited copies in their
> institutional repositories, which a search on the DOI will find.

**Richardson, K., et al. (2023).** Earth beyond six of nine planetary boundaries.
*Science Advances*, 9(37), eadh2458. <https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adh2458>

Source of the planetary boundaries figure used in the session.

- Download: [richardson-2023-earth-beyond-six-of-nine-planetary-boundaries.pdf](richardson-2023-earth-beyond-six-of-nine-planetary-boundaries.pdf)
- Open access, CC BY-NC 4.0. Copyright 2023 the authors, exclusive licensee AAAS.

## Optional

**Ceballos, G., et al. (2015).** Accelerated modern human-induced species losses: Entering the sixth
mass extinction. *Science Advances*, 1(5), e1400253. <https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1400253>

Short, and deliberately conservative in its assumptions, which is what makes its conclusion hard to
argue with.

- Download: [ceballos-2015-accelerated-modern-human-induced-species-losses.pdf](ceballos-2015-accelerated-modern-human-induced-species-losses.pdf)
- Open access, CC BY-NC 4.0. Copyright 2015 the authors, exclusive licensee AAAS.

## Recommended

Pievani, T., and Belardinelli, S. (2023). Mass Extinction. In N. Wallenhorst and C. Wulf (eds.),
*Handbook of the Anthropocene* (pp. 263-269). Springer.

Testot, L. (2023). Megafaunal Extinction. In N. Wallenhorst and C. Wulf (eds.),
*Handbook of the Anthropocene* (pp. 271-277). Springer.

## Background for the whole course

*The Handbook of the Anthropocene* (Springer, 2023).
